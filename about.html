About me

I didn't take the usual path into engineering. I started at Emory in the dual-degree program with Georgia Tech and spent three years studying East Asian Studies, mostly Japanese language and culture. I finished that degree in May 2026 with a 3.95 GPA and started at Tech this fall. Learning a language as an adult taught me to put up with not understanding something for a long time before it clicks, and that turned out to be good preparation for debugging hardware.

My first engineering internship was at Collins Aerospace (RTX) in summer 2025. I built a Python tool that used an LLM, retrieval, and deterministic tool-calling to generate flight-plan changes and simulate abnormal flight scenarios for avionics testing. It was interesting work, but I kept finding myself more curious about the aircraft systems than about the language model. I wanted to know what was happening at the level of timing, voltages, and physics.

So in summer 2026 I split my time. During the day I was a remote software intern at Kashmir World Foundation, building an agent that controls a gimbaled camera on a simulated UAV. On my own time (and between shifts at a part-time housekeeping job) I taught myself bare-metal C on an STM32 and built a pan/tilt robot that tracks a hand with a camera. It was the first project where I really had to dig into the math and physics underneath, and the hardest bug looked like a tuning problem but was actually a timing problem between the camera and the encoders. Fixing it was the most satisfying thing I'd done in engineering, and it's a big part of why I want to do firmware work.

At Georgia Tech I'm pursuing the Computing Hardware & Emerging Architectures and Robotics & Autonomous Systems threads. I'm also on the Autobots VIP research team working on navigation in dynamic environments, and I write firmware for the Marine Robotics Club's autonomous surface robot.
