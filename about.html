About me

Before coming to Georgia Tech, I spent three years studying humanities and STEM fundamentals at Emory University as part of a 3+2 dual-degree. I finished the first part of the program in May 2026 and started at Tech this fall.

My first engineering internship was at Collins Aerospace (RTX) in summer 2025. I built a Python tool that used an LLM, retrieval, and deterministic tool-calling to generate flight-plan changes and simulate abnormal flight scenarios for avionics testing. It was interesting work, but I kept finding myself more curious about the aircraft systems than about the language model. I wanted to know what was happening at the physical level, and how engineers leverage that to make hardware sense and respond to stuff happening around it.

So in summer 2026 I split my time: During the day I was a remote software intern at Kashmir World Foundation, building an agent that controls a gimbaled camera on a simulated UAV. On my own time I taught myself bare-metal C on an STM32 and built a pan/tilt robot that tracks a hand with a camera. It was the first project where I really had to dig into the math and physics underneath. Working on that project was the most satisfying thing I'd done in engineering, and it's a big part of why I want to pursue firmware, especially in controls and signal processing contexts.

At Georgia Tech I'm pursuing the Computing Hardware & Emerging Architectures and Robotics & Autonomous Systems threads. I'm also on the Autobots VIP research team working on navigation in dynamic environments, and I write firmware for a brand new club called WEAR @ GT (Wearable Exoskeletons an Assitive Robotics at Georgia Tech).
